Architecture Guide

Engines vs. Models: What's the Difference?

In Aura, execution is cleanly decoupled so you always know exactly how your Mac's hardware is being utilized:

The Engine (The Runtime Pipeline)

The software backend executing tensor mathematics on your Mac. Aura uses Apple MLX to tap Apple Silicon unified memory directly via Metal. You can also switch to Apple Intelligence (macOS 15+) or connect to an existing Local Server (LM Studio / Ollama).

The Model (The Neural Weights)

The downloaded quantized weights that determine reasoning depth and vocabulary. Pick Balanced (1.82 GB) for ~1s proofreading, Writer (3.9 GB Gemma 3n) for nuanced human tone, or Expert (8.36 GB) for high-parameter excellence.

💡 RAM Management: Keep your model loaded in memory for instant hotkey rewrites, or click Unload Model in the Control Panel anytime to immediately release system RAM.

What you write on your Mac, stays on your Mac.

Aura has no account, no sign-in, and zero analytics tracking. Your text is rewritten exclusively by a model executing in your Mac's unified memory via Apple MLX and the Neural Engine. Zero tokens or drafts are ever uploaded, cached to third-party disks, or sent to any server — ours or anyone else's.

How 0 KB Network Egress is Guaranteed

When you press ⇧⌘P, Aura uses standard macOS Accessibility APIs to read the selected string directly into app memory. By default, the text is passed into Aura's own local MLX inference engine running on your GPU, generated tokens stream back to the UI, and the final output is written back — no network sockets are opened during inference. You can inspect Aura with Little Snitch, LuLu, or Wireshark to verify it. (If you choose the optional Local Server engine to talk to LM Studio or Ollama, that connects only to loopback addresses on your own Mac — never the internet.)
